Broadstone housing, and what each type needs

Broadstone grew up around its railway junction on the old Somerset and Dorset line. The track closed in the 1960s and is now the Castleman Trailway, but the housing it left behind is why the electrical work here looks different to the rest of Poole.

  • Bungalows, and lots of them - whole roads, mostly 1950s to 1970s. Frequently one owner for thirty years or more, so the board and the socket count are stuck in the decade the house was built.
  • Big Edwardian and 1930s houses up by the golf course. Solid walls, picture rails, deep skirtings. The wiring has usually been done once, in the 70s or 80s, and is now due again.
  • Post-war semis off Lower Blandford Road and Dunyeats Road. Sound cable as a rule, so the job is normally the board and a few extra circuits rather than a full rewire.

Broadstone also sits high, on some genuinely steep roads. On a sloping plot the meter and the board are often in an odd spot - a raised side entrance, a garage below the house - and that decides where the new board can sensibly go.

Bungalow rewires - Broadstone's speciality

Broadstone has one of the biggest stocks of bungalows in the area, and a bungalow is one of the cleanest rewires there is. With a full loft over every room, most of the new cabling runs through the loft instead of under floors - so there is less lifting, less chasing and less making-good than in a two-storey house.

A lot of Broadstone bungalows are bought from long-term owners and modernised by the next one. If that is you, the order matters: rewire first, before the plastering, the new kitchen and the decorating - not after. An empty, just-bought bungalow is the perfect rewire, and it is done before you move in.

What one long ownership does to the wiring

The usual suspects, and where they turn up:

  • A 1970s or 80s board with no RCD - the standard find in a bungalow that has had one careful owner since it was built
  • Rubber or fabric-covered cable in the older detached houses near the golf course, brittle enough to crumble when it is moved
  • One socket a wall, because that was plenty in 1965 and is nowhere near enough now
  • An immersion heater on its own ancient circuit, often the oldest wiring left in the house
  • Loft cable resting on the joists under new insulation - very common where lofts were topped up for the grant schemes and nobody lifted the cable clear
  • Warm plugs, scorch marks or a buzz from the board - stop using it and call us

Not sure which of these matter in your home? An EICR safety report puts it in writing, with every problem graded by how urgent it really is.

Working in Broadstone, and who signs it off

Broadstone is part of Poole, so it comes under BCP Council. Cross the boundary to Wimborne or Corfe Mullen and it is Dorset Council instead. Anything notifiable gets registered with whichever one covers your address. That is our job, not yours, and your certificate follows without you chasing it.

Two practical things about working here:

  • Steep roads and long driveways up the golf course side. Fine for the van, but it can mean a long carry from the road, so we allow for it rather than surprising you with it.
  • The Broadway flats - parking is time-limited and there is no loading bay outside most of them. We unload first, then move the van, the same as any town centre.

Do you have to move out?

For a full rewire, yes. We only rewire empty properties. The boards come up and the walls get opened, so an empty home is faster and cheaper for you.

In Broadstone that fits the market almost perfectly. A large share of the bungalows here change hands after a long single ownership, often as probate sales, and get modernised straight away. If that is your situation the order matters: rewire first, then plaster, then the kitchen, then decorate. Doing it the other way round means cutting into work you have just paid for.

For smaller jobs you stay put. A board swap, an EICR or an EV charger is a day or less. If the cable is sound and only the board is dated, a consumer unit upgrade may be all you need.

Quick answers

Do you cover my part of Broadstone?

Yes. We cover all of Broadstone and the BH18 postcodes, including the Broadway, the roads up by the golf course, Springdale and the Dunyeats Road area, plus nearby Merley, Corfe Mullen, Creekmoor and Canford Heath. There is no call-out charge for Broadstone.

How much does a rewire cost in Broadstone?

Most rewires run from about £2,800 for a 1-bed flat to around £11,000 for a 4-bed house. There is no VAT to add. Your price is fixed in writing after a free survey. See the cost guide for the full breakdown.

Do you rewire bungalows?

Yes, a lot of them - Broadstone is full of bungalows. A single-storey home with a full loft above it is one of the cleanest rewires there is: most cables run through the loft, so there is less chasing and less disruption. If you have just bought a dated bungalow to modernise, rewiring it before you move in is the ideal time.

Is Broadstone under BCP or Dorset Council?

BCP Council, because Broadstone is part of Poole. Wimborne and Corfe Mullen, just up the road, are Dorset Council. Whichever one covers your address is the one we register the work with, and we sort that ourselves.

We are selling Mum's bungalow. What do we actually need?

Usually an EICR, not a rewire. It gives you a written report on what is wrong and how urgent each item is, which is exactly what a buyer's surveyor will ask about. If it comes back clear you have proof. If it does not, you can decide whether to fix it or price it in - with a fixed quote in writing either way.

Do I have to move out?

Only for a full rewire, which we do on empty properties. A fuse box swap, a safety report or a charge point takes a day at most, with you still living there.

Are you qualified, and is there a guarantee?

Yes. We are Part P registered, and every rewire comes with a 10-year workmanship guarantee in writing.
