Why Ferndown homes need rewiring

Ferndown grew fast through the 1960s, 70s and 80s, so a lot of its housing is now that age - plus pockets of older stock. Wiring and fuseboards from that era are often past their best. Plenty of homes still have an old board with no RCD protection, or the original cabling behind the walls. Once a home is 25 to 30 years past its last rewire, the cables and the board are usually due a look.

Homes bought to renovate or extend are a common reason people call us. If you have just bought a dated house near Tricketts Cross or West Parley, an empty-property rewire before you move in is the clean, cheap way to do it.

If you are not sure whether you need anything done, we will do a free survey and a quick on-the-spot check. If the wiring is fine, we will tell you.

Signs your home needs a rewire

A few things that usually mean it is time for a proper look:

  • An old fusebox with rewireable fuses or no test buttons (no RCD protection)
  • Old rubber- or fabric-covered cables instead of modern PVC - the insulation goes brittle and cracks with age
  • Breakers that keep tripping or fuses that keep blowing
  • Sockets that scorch, buzz or feel warm
  • Not enough sockets, so extension leads are everywhere
  • Flickering lights or wiring that has never been checked

Not sure which of these matter? An EICR safety report gives you a written answer.

Do you have to move out?

For a full rewire, yes - we rewire empty properties only. It is faster, cleaner and cheaper than working around you, and it means no dust and disruption in a home you are living in. It suits a house you have just bought, a renovation or extension, or a probate or rental property between tenants.

Smaller jobs are different. A consumer unit upgrade, an EICR, an EV charger or extra sockets are all done while you live in the home, on a day that suits. If the wiring is sound and only the fusebox is dated, you may not need a full rewire at all - just a consumer unit upgrade. An EICR tells you which it is.
