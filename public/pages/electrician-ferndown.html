The Ferndown house that kept growing

Ferndown has a lot of big detached homes on generous plots, and generous plots invite extensions. Most houses we look at here are not one house electrically. They are an original house plus a conservatory, plus a garage conversion, plus a loft room, each added in a different decade by a different pair of hands.

That shows up in three ways:

  • A board with no room left. The original had eight ways and the house now wants fourteen. Everything since has been squeezed onto a spare or spurred off something else.
  • Circuits nobody can account for. A cable disappears into a wall and reappears in the conservatory, and there is no certificate for any of it.
  • Loads the house was never designed for. An electric shower, an induction hob, a hot tub and a car charger were not on anyone's mind when the main supply was sized.

Before quoting a rewire on a house like this we work out what the supply and the board can actually carry. Sometimes the answer is that you do not need a rewire at all, just a bigger board and two new circuits.

Annexes, garden offices and granny flats

The other job this town produces more than anywhere else. Big plots and multi-generational families mean a lot of Ferndown homes have somewhere separate that someone actually lives or works in.

That is a different job to running power to a shed, and worth knowing before you plan it:

  • Somewhere lived in needs its own consumer unit and its own means of isolation, not a socket circuit stretched down the garden.
  • The supply has to be sized for it, including whatever heats it in January.
  • It usually needs its own certification, and a buyer's solicitor will ask for that paperwork later even if nobody asks now.

We will tell you honestly whether what you want fits the supply you have, before you commit to the building work.

What we find in a Ferndown house

The recurring ones:

  • A full board with nothing spare - the reason a charger or a shower turns into a bigger job than expected
  • Extension wiring joined into the original circuits with no paperwork for any of it
  • An early consumer unit from the original build - working, but without the protection a modern board gives
  • Old rubber or fabric-covered cable in the pre-boom pockets, brittle and cracking
  • A garden building on a lead through a window, which is where an awful lot of garden offices start
  • Sockets that scorch, buzz or feel warm - stop using them and call us

Not sure which of these matter? An EICR safety report gives you a written answer, graded by urgency.

Do you have to move out?

For a full rewire, yes - we rewire empty properties only. It is dusty, noisy work with the power off, and it goes faster with nobody in. It suits a house just bought, a renovation, or a property between tenants.

Smaller jobs are different. A consumer unit upgrade, an EICR, an EV charger or extra sockets are all done while you live in the home, on a day that suits. If the wiring is sound and only the fusebox is dated, you may not need a full rewire at all - just a consumer unit upgrade. An EICR tells you which it is.

I want a garden office. What does the power side involve?

More than most people expect, and it is worth knowing before the building goes up. Somewhere you actually work needs its own consumer unit and its own isolation, a supply sized for whatever heats it in winter, and its own certificate. A lead out through a window is not it. My board is full. Do I need a whole rewire?

Usually not. On a Ferndown house that has been extended a few times, the cable is often fine and the board is the bottleneck. A bigger board plus the circuits you actually need is a fraction of a rewire. The free survey tells you which one you are looking at.
