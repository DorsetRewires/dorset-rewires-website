Wimborne housing, and what each type needs

Wimborne splits into three kinds of home. Each one throws up a different job, and a different price.

  • The conservation area - Georgian and Victorian townhouses around the Minster, the Square and West Borough. Lath-and-plaster walls, solid floors, no cavity. Cables run under the boards or in surface conduit, because cutting into a listed wall is often not allowed.
  • Colehill and the older edges - interwar and post-war houses and bungalows on generous plots. Plenty still have a rewireable fuse box in the hall cupboard and sockets set into the skirting.
  • Merley and Canford Bottom - 1960s and 1970s estate housing. The cable is usually sound, so the job is normally a board swap, not a rewire: an early breaker board with no RCD, and nowhere near enough sockets for how people live now.

Out at Pamphill and Stapehill it changes again. Thick cob and stone walls, long runs out to outbuildings, and garden supplies that were added years ago without much thought.

One more Wimborne thing. The Stour and the Allen meet just below the town. If your home has ever taken water, we mount the board and the sockets higher than standard, and we tell you why before we start.

What we find behind Wimborne front doors

The common ones, and where we usually find them:

  • A rewireable fuse box with porcelain fuse carriers and no test button - still common in Colehill and the older town-centre houses
  • An early breaker board with no RCD - the classic Merley and Canford Bottom find, fitted decades ago and never touched since
  • Black rubber or fabric-covered cable under the floorboards, which goes brittle and crumbles as soon as it is moved
  • Two sockets to a room, with extension leads doing the rest of the work
  • Surface conduit added over the years in the listed properties, where nobody wanted to cut the walls about
  • Warm plugs, scorch marks or a buzz from the board - stop using it and call us

Not sure which of these matter in your home? An EICR safety report puts it in writing, with each problem graded by how urgent it is.

Working in the town centre

The centre is tight, and it changes how we plan a job.

  • Parking - the Square and the streets off it have loading restrictions. We unload, then move the van to a car park, so allow a few extra minutes each morning.
  • No side access - the terraces on East Street and West Borough often have none, so everything comes in through the front door and the floors get covered before we start.
  • Shared walls - on a terrace we agree the noisy work in advance, so your neighbours get some warning about the chasing.

Listed and conservation-area homes

A lot of central Wimborne is listed or sits inside the conservation area. That is not a problem, but it does change the job.

Cutting into walls, ceilings or floors in a listed building can need listed building consent. We check before we quote, not after we have started. Where consent would be a fight, we route cables under floors or in surface conduit instead, and we show you where it will run before you commit.

It usually costs a little more in labour than a modern house of the same size. We put that in the quote up front, so the number does not move later.

Who signs the work off

Wimborne sits in Dorset Council. Broadstone and Poole, a few miles south, are BCP Council. Notifiable electrical work has to be registered with the right one.

We handle that paperwork either way. You get your certificate without chasing anybody.

Do you have to move out?

For a full rewire, yes. We only rewire empty properties. The boards come up and the walls get opened, so an empty house is faster and cheaper for you.

That suits Wimborne well. A lot of the rewires here are on houses bought to do up, or probate sales being cleared before they go back on the market. Get it done before the furniture arrives.

For smaller jobs you stay put. A board swap, an EICR or an EV charger is a day or less, done around you. If the cable is sound and only the board is dated - the usual answer in Merley - a consumer unit upgrade may be all you need.

Quick answers

Do you cover my part of Wimborne?

Yes. All of BH21 - the town centre, Colehill, Merley, Canford Bottom, Pamphill and Stapehill. There is no call-out charge for Wimborne.

My house is listed. Can you still rewire it?

Yes, and a good part of central Wimborne is listed or in the conservation area. Cutting into walls, ceilings or floors may need listed building consent, so we check that before we quote. Where consent would be difficult we run cables under floors or in surface conduit instead, and show you the route before you commit.

How much does a rewire cost in Wimborne?

Most rewires run from about £2,800 for a 1-bed flat to around £11,000 for a 4-bed house. There is no VAT to add. Your price is fixed in writing after a free survey. See the cost guide for the full breakdown.

My house is 1970s. Do I need a full rewire?

Often not. In Merley and Canford Bottom the cable is usually still sound, and the real problem is the board and the socket count. A fuse box swap plus a few extra circuits fixes it for a lot less than a rewire. The free survey tells you which one you actually need.

Is Wimborne under Dorset Council or BCP?

Dorset Council. Broadstone and Poole are BCP. Notifiable work has to be registered with the right one, and we handle that paperwork either way.

Do I have to move out?

Only for a full rewire, which we do on empty properties. A board swap, an EICR or an EV charger is done around you in a day or less.
