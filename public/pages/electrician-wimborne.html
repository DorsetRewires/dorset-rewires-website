Why Wimborne homes need rewiring

Wimborne has a lot of older and characterful housing - period cottages and townhouses around the Minster and the Square, along with post-war estates in Colehill, Merley and Canford Bottom. Plenty of these homes still have their original wiring and an old fusebox with no RCD protection.

Once a home is 25 to 30 years past its last rewire, the cables and the board are usually due a look. Rubber or fabric-covered cable, a fuse box with rewireable fuses, and not enough sockets are all common in the older stock here.

If you are not sure whether you need anything done, we will do a free survey and a quick on-the-spot check. If the wiring is fine, we will tell you.

Signs your home needs a rewire

A few tells that the wiring in a Wimborne home is past its best:

  • An old fuse box with rewireable fuses or no test buttons (no RCD protection)
  • Sockets set into the skirting boards, or a home that has not been rewired in decades
  • Cables in black rubber or fabric instead of modern grey or white PVC
  • Breakers that keep tripping, or sockets and lights that are dead or flickering
  • Too few sockets, so extension leads are doing the work
  • Warm plugs, scorch marks or a buzzing sound - stop and call an electrician

Not sure which of these matter? An EICR safety report tells you what is wrong and how urgent it is, in writing.

Do you have to move out?

For a full rewire, yes - we rewire empty properties only. Floorboards come up, walls get chased and the power goes off, so an empty house is faster, cleaner and cheaper than working around you. It is ideal for a house you have just bought, a renovation or extension, or a probate or rental property between tenants. Many Wimborne homes are bought to do up, which suits this perfectly - get the rewire done before you move in.

For smaller jobs you stay put. A new consumer unit, an EICR, an EV charger or a few extra sockets are all done around you, on a day that suits. If the wiring is sound and only the fusebox is dated, you may not need a full rewire at all - just a consumer unit upgrade.
