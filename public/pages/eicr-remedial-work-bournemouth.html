The 28-day clock - what the law actually says

If the property is rented and the report is unsatisfactory, the rules are strict and dated:

  • 28 days to do the work. C1 and C2 faults (and any FI items) must be put right within 28 days of the inspection - or sooner if the report sets an earlier date
  • Written confirmation. Once the work is done, the landlord needs written confirmation from a qualified person that it meets the standard, and must give it to the tenant within 28 days of the work finishing - and to the council if they asked for it
  • Real fines. Councils can fine landlords up to £40,000 per breach - the maximum rose from £30,000 with effect from 1 May 2026, and the rules now extend to the social rented sector. The council can also arrange the work itself and bill you

None of that is meant to scare you. It is meant to show why "we will get round to it" is not a plan. Send the report over today and the clock stops being a problem.

Your codes, decoded

The report lists every problem with a code. Only some of them make it a fail:

  • C1 - danger present. A live risk of injury, right now. It should have been made safe on the day it was found. If your tester left it live, call us today - that is not acceptable and we will deal with it first
  • C2 - potentially dangerous. The main reason reports fail. Must be fixed for the installation to be signed off as safe
  • FI - further investigation. Something the tester could not prove safe. It counts as a fail until it is investigated and resolved
  • C3 - improvement recommended. Not a fail. A sensible upgrade, entirely your choice. Nobody should pressure you into C3 work to "pass"

Send us the report and we will tell you in plain English which items actually stand between you and a satisfactory result - and which are optional extras someone would like to sell you.

Quick answers

Can you fix a failed EICR that another company did?

Yes. Send us the report - a photo on WhatsApp is fine - and we quote every C1, C2 and FI item in writing. You do not need to use the company that tested. The remedial work is yours to place with anyone.

How long do landlords have to fix a failed EICR?

28 days, or sooner if the report sets an earlier date. The landlord must then get written confirmation that the work is done and give it to the tenant within 28 days of the work finishing, and to the council if they asked for it.

What does EICR remedial work cost?

Small fixes start at our £150 minimum visit, which covers the first hour - extra time is £50 an hour, agreed as we go. A damaged socket is typically £90. A new consumer unit is £625 fitted. Bigger jobs get a fixed written quote before we start. There is no VAT to add.

Do I need a whole new EICR after the remedial work?

Usually not. We certify the work we carry out, and that certificate is the written confirmation the landlord rules ask for. If we did your original EICR, the re-test within 30 days of our remedial work is free.

What happens if I ignore an unsatisfactory EICR?

For a rented property the council can serve a remedial notice, arrange the work themselves and bill you, and fine up to £40,000 per breach. The maximum rose from £30,000 on 1 May 2026, and the rules now extend to the social rented sector.
