Air Conditioning Cost Guide - Dorset 2026

Last updated: July 2026

Installed prices in 2026

National figures for supplied-and-installed systems, from the major cost-guide surveys this year:

  • Single-room wall split - most homeowners pay £1,600 to £2,800; budget systems from around £1,500, premium brands in awkward spots up to £3,000
  • Two-room multi-split - typically £2,800 to £4,500
  • Three to four rooms - typically £3,500 to £7,000

Our own quotes are fixed in writing after a free survey, with no VAT to add and no markup on materials. We are building a transparent price-it-yourself calculator for air conditioning like the one we run for rewires - until it is live, the survey and written quote do the same honest job.

What moves the price

  • Unit brand and size - the biggest single factor. A quality 2.5 kilowatt bedroom unit and a premium 5 kilowatt living-room unit are hundreds of pounds apart at trade
  • Pipe-run length and route - back-to-back installs (unit directly behind the outdoor position) are the cheapest; long runs through lofts or around elevations add pipe, trunking and time
  • Wall construction and access - a core drill through brick is routine; solid stone, high walls or scaffold needs are not
  • The electrical work - every install needs a dedicated circuit and isolator, and sometimes fuse board work. This is our own trade, included in the fixed price - on other quotes it is often an exclusion in the small print
  • Condensate route - gravity drains are simple; where a pump is needed, it is priced in from the start, not discovered later

Running costs: cooling and heating

Cooling: a typical 2.5 kilowatt bedroom unit draws well under a kilowatt in normal use - roughly 15p to 25p an hour at current electricity prices. Run it every summer night for a month and you have spent less than a takeaway.

Heating is where the sums get interesting. Every modern wall split is a reversible air-to-air heat pump delivering 3 to 4 kilowatts of heat per kilowatt of electricity. Against plug-in panel or storage heaters, that is roughly a 70 percent cut in running cost for the same warmth - transformative for flats, conservatories, garden offices and any home without mains gas. Against a gas boiler it is broadly at parity on a standard tariff, and cheaper on smart time-of-use tariffs.

The winter trick

Air conditioning is a summer purchase with a winter price. Demand collapses outside the heatwaves, installer diaries open up, and pricing typically runs 5 to 10 percent keener between October and March. Buy in the cool months and you get the sharper price, no waiting list, and a unit that immediately starts earning as cheap room heating - then summer arrives already sorted.

How to compare quotes

  • Is the electrical circuit included? A dedicated circuit is required. If the quote does not mention it, it is probably an extra
  • Is the number plus VAT? Many are. Ours has no VAT to add at all
  • Who does the refrigerant work? The law requires an F-Gas certified engineer for the refrigerant connections and commissioning - ask, and expect a straight answer. On our installs that work is completed by an F-Gas certified engineer and the manufacturer's warranty is registered on completion
  • Is the noise assessment done? Permitted development depends on a noise check to the current MCS standard. A proper installer does it at the survey, not after a neighbour complains
  • Is the price fixed? Ours is: the written figure only changes if you change the job

Quick answers

How much does air conditioning cost to install in the UK in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between £1,600 and £2,800 for a single-room wall split, supplied and installed. Budget systems start around £1,500 and premium brands in awkward locations can reach £3,000. Multi-room systems typically run £3,500 to £7,000.

How much does air conditioning cost to run?

A typical 2.5 kilowatt bedroom unit costs roughly 15p to 25p an hour in cooling mode at current electricity prices. In heating mode it delivers about 3 to 4 kilowatts of heat per kilowatt of electricity, which works out around 70 percent cheaper than plug-in electric heaters for the same warmth.

Is it cheaper to install air conditioning in winter?

Usually yes - demand drops outside the summer, diaries open up and pricing is typically 5 to 10 percent keener. Winter is also when the heating side of the unit pays for itself, so an autumn or winter install is the smart-money timing.

Why do quotes vary so much between installers?

Unit brand and size, pipe-run length, wall construction, access, and whether the electrical work is included or quietly left out. Always check the quote covers the dedicated electrical circuit, the condensate route and the commissioning paperwork - ours always do, as one fixed written price.

Does the price include VAT?

Ours has no VAT to add at all - we are not VAT registered, so the written figure is the figure you pay. When comparing quotes, check whether a competitor's number is plus VAT; many are.
