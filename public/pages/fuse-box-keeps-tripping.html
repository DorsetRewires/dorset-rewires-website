Safe right now - do these 3 things first

  1. Do not hold the switch on. A trip is the safety system working. Forcing it defeats the one thing protecting you
  2. Unplug, then reset once. Unplug everything on the circuit that drops. Reset the switch. If it holds, plug things back in one at a time - the one that trips it is your suspect. Stop there and call us
  3. Burning smell or scorch marks? Stop. Turn the whole board off at the big main switch and call us now on 07836 535100. That one is not a wait-and-see

If the switch will not reset at all, leave that circuit off. The rest of the house can stay on. You are safe - just stuck. That is the visit we exist for.

What the tripping is telling you

Your fuse box has two kinds of switch, and which one drops narrows the hunt:

  • One small switch drops - a circuit breaker. One circuit is overloaded or has a damaged cable or accessory. Usual suspects: a cooked connection behind a socket, a nail or screw through a cable, or simply too much plugged into one ring
  • A wider switch drops and takes half the house - the safety switch (RCD). Electricity is leaking somewhere it should not be. Usual suspects: a failing appliance (fridges, ovens, washing machines and heaters top the list), water in an outside socket or light, or a cable that has been damp for years
  • It only trips in the rain, or at night, or randomly - that is an intermittent fault. It is the type most firms will not touch, and the type we enjoy. More on that below

You do not need to work any of this out yourself. Tell us what drops and when - that is all we need to start.

The fault that only happens sometimes

Intermittent faults are the worst to live with and the hardest to sell by the hour - which is why so many firms swerve them. Being straight with you:

  • Most are found on the first visit. A methodical hunt finds the damp junction box or the dying appliance that "random" tripping usually turns out to be
  • Some genuinely need a second look. If yours needs monitoring equipment left in place, or a return visit to catch it in the act, we agree that with you in writing before anything else happens
  • The fee still never grows mid-visit. You will never get a surprise number at the door. That rule does not bend

Why nobody else calls you back

You have probably already discovered the strange truth about tripping problems: electricians go quiet on them. It is not personal - it is maths:

  • An unknown fault is an open-ended job. On an hourly rate, that is a risk for the electrician and a blank cheque for you. Neither side likes it, so the job never gets booked
  • Small diagnostic jobs lose to big installs. A day of chasing a fault pays less than a day fitting a fuse board, so fault-finding sinks to the bottom of every to-do list
  • Meanwhile you are resetting a switch every night - and the cause, whatever it is, is not getting better on its own

Quick answers

How much does electrical fault finding cost?

£149, fixed. That covers the visit, the diagnosis, a written answer and a fixed price for the repair. If we do the repair, the £149 comes off the bill. There is no hourly meter and no VAT to add.

My electrics keep tripping - is it dangerous?

A trip is the safety system doing its job, so do not just hold the switch on. Leave the tripping circuit off if it will not reset. If you smell burning or see scorch marks, turn the whole board off at the main switch and call us straight away.

Can you find a fault that only happens sometimes?

Yes - intermittent faults are the ones we like. Most are found on the first visit. If yours genuinely needs monitoring equipment or a return visit, we agree that with you in writing first. The £149 fee never grows mid-visit.

Why do other electricians turn down fault-finding jobs?

Because on an hourly rate an unknown fault is a risk - for them and for you. Nobody wants an open-ended bill, so many firms just do not pick up the phone for tripping problems. Our fixed price moves that risk onto us.

Will you tell me I need a rewire?

Almost certainly not. Most tripping problems end in a small repair - a damaged cable, a faulty accessory, water where it should not be, or one failing appliance. If the fault genuinely points at a bigger problem, we show you the evidence in writing and the decision stays yours.
