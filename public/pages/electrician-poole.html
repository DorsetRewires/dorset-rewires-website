Why Poole homes need an electrician

Poole runs from Edwardian terraces in Parkstone to 1930s detached homes in Branksome Park, plus post-war bungalows and a lot of newer estates. Older homes often still have their original wiring and a fusebox with no RCD protection. Pete knows these houses well - he has rewired period homes across Poole, including his own 1880s cottage in Parkstone, which had a rewireable fusebox when he moved in.

The coast matters here too. Salt air around Sandbanks and the harbour corrodes outdoor sockets, lights and isolators faster than inland, so we fit weatherproof, marine-grade gear where it is needed. If you are not sure whether you need anything, we will do a free survey and a quick on-the-spot check.

Signs your home needs electrical work

A few things that usually mean it is worth a proper look:

  • An old fusebox with rewireable fuses or no test buttons (no RCD protection)
  • Sockets set into the skirting boards - common in Poole Old Town cottages and the Victorian terraces of Parkstone (Pete rewired his own 1880s Parkstone cottage)
  • Old rubber- or fabric-covered cables instead of modern PVC - the insulation goes brittle and cracks with age. The 1930s semis of Branksome and the 1960s-70s homes of Creekmoor and Upton are the usual suspects
  • Breakers that keep tripping or fuses that keep blowing
  • Sockets that scorch, buzz or feel warm
  • Not enough sockets, so extension leads are everywhere
  • Flickering lights or wiring that has never been checked

Not sure which of these matter? An EICR safety report gives you a written answer.

Do you have to move out?

For a full rewire, yes - we rewire empty properties only. It is faster, cleaner and cheaper than working around you, and it means no dust in a home you are living in. It suits a house you have just bought, a renovation, or a probate or rental property between tenants.

Everything else is done while you live in. A consumer unit upgrade, an EICR, an EV charger or extra sockets are all done around you, on a day that suits. If the wiring is sound and only the fusebox is dated, you may not need a full rewire at all - just a new consumer unit. An EICR tells you which it is.
