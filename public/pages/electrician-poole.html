This is where Pete lives

Poole is not a service area on a map to us. Pete lives in Parkstone, and the first house he rewired properly was his own: an 1880s cottage that still had a rewireable fuse box the day he got the keys. Lath and plaster, no earth on the lighting, and floorboards that had been up and down for a century.

Two practical things follow from being based here:

  • We can usually look the same week. Poole is not a drive out, it is the doorstep, so a survey is easy to fit in.
  • We already know the housing. When somebody says a Parkstone terrace or a Creekmoor semi, we know what is behind the wall before we get there, and the quote reflects that instead of padding for the unknown.

Poole changes completely in three miles

No other town near here covers this much ground. What your home needs depends far more on which bit of Poole it is in than on its age alone.

  • Old Town and Parkstone - Victorian and Edwardian cottages and terraces. Solid walls, no cavity, sockets in the skirting. Cable runs under floors rather than through them, and making good is a real part of the job.
  • Branksome, Penn Hill and Lilliput - 1930s detached on larger plots. Usually rewired once already, in the 70s or 80s, which means it is due again now rather than urgent.
  • Canford Heath, Creekmoor and Upton - 1960s to 80s estate housing. The cable is often sound. The board and the socket count are what have aged, so a full rewire is frequently the wrong answer here.
  • Hamworthy and the quay side - a real mix, plus flats and conversions where working out what feeds what comes before any price.

We tell you which of those you are, and what it honestly needs, at the free survey.

What we find in Poole homes

The recurring ones, by area:

  • A rewireable fuse box still in service - Pete had one in his own Parkstone cottage, so this is not a rare find here
  • No earth on the lighting circuit in the pre-war stock, which matters the moment anyone fits a metal fitting
  • Sockets in the skirting in the Old Town and Parkstone terraces
  • Brittle rubber or fabric cable in the 1930s houses of Branksome and Penn Hill
  • A sound but undersized installation on the Canford Heath and Creekmoor estates - a board problem, not a rewire
  • Sockets that scorch, buzz or feel warm - stop using them and call us

Not sure which of these matter? An EICR safety report settles it in writing, with each item graded so you know what is urgent and what can wait.

Do you have to move out?

Only for a full rewire, and only because we work on empty properties. It is dusty work with the power off, and it finishes quicker with nobody in. A just-bought house before the furniture arrives is the ideal.

Everything smaller happens with you still there. A new consumer unit, a safety check, a charge point or a few sockets are a day at most. And on a lot of Poole housing that is the whole answer: sound cable, tired board. An EICR tells you which camp you are in before you spend anything.
