What air conditioning really is now

Every modern wall-split unit is a reversible air-to-air heat pump. In summer it cools and dehumidifies. In winter it runs in reverse and heats the room, delivering roughly 3 to 4 kilowatts of heat for every kilowatt of electricity it uses. That makes it about 70 percent cheaper to run than plug-in electric heaters for the same warmth - which is why the smart way to buy one is as a year-round appliance, not a summer gadget.

Fewer than 5 in 100 UK homes have one, while around half of UK homes now overheat in summer. Dorset's mild coastal winters also mean the heating side works at its most efficient exactly where we live.

The systems we fit

  • Single wall split - one indoor unit, one outdoor unit. The bedroom, living room or home office answer, typically fitted in a day
  • Multi-split - one outdoor unit running two to four indoor units. Whole-upstairs cooling or several rooms without a row of boxes outside
  • Garden rooms, offices and conservatories - spaces that are unusable in August and freezing in January are exactly what a heating-and-cooling unit fixes
  • Flats and annexes - efficient room heating where there is no gas, without storage heaters

How the install works

Every install is a two-trade job. Our electricians handle the survey, mounting, pipework routing, condensate, wiring and testing - the half most air conditioning firms subcontract out. The refrigerant connections, pressure test and commissioning are completed by an F-Gas certified engineer, and the manufacturer's warranty is registered on completion. One firm, one boss: Pete quotes the job, runs the job and stays your single point of contact.

Being electrician-led matters more than it sounds: every air conditioning install needs a dedicated electrical circuit, an isolator and a fuse board with the capacity to take it. For most installers that is a subcontract. For us it is the day job, tested and certificated properly.

Planning permission, in plain English

The rules changed in May 2025, in your favour. A unit that provides heating as well as cooling - which every unit we fit does - is permitted development on most houses: no planning application, provided the outdoor unit is under 1.5 cubic metres, it is not on a wall facing the road, and there are no more than two units on a detached house or one otherwise. A noise assessment to the current MCS standard applies, and we complete it as part of the survey. Listed buildings and cooling-only units still need a planning application - we tell you straight at the survey if yours is one of them.

What it costs to run

A typical 2.5 kilowatt bedroom unit costs roughly 15p to 25p an hour to run in cooling mode at current electricity prices. In heating mode the same unit delivers warmth at a running cost broadly on a par with mains gas - and far below any plug-in heater. Nationally, most homeowners pay between £1,600 and £2,800 for a single-room system supplied and installed; multi-room systems run £3,500 to £7,000. Quick answers

Does air conditioning heat as well as cool?

Yes. Every modern wall-split unit is a reversible air-to-air heat pump: it cools in summer and heats in winter, typically delivering 3 to 4 kilowatts of heat for every kilowatt of electricity. Run against plug-in electric heaters, that is roughly a 70 percent cut in running cost for the same warmth.

Do I need planning permission for air conditioning in England?

Usually no. Since May 2025, units that provide heating as well as cooling are permitted development on most houses: up to 1.5 cubic metres, not on a wall facing the road, up to two units on a detached house and one otherwise. Cooling-only units and listed buildings still need a planning application, and a noise check to the current MCS standard applies. We check your property as part of the free survey.

How much does air conditioning cost to install?

Who does the refrigerant work?

How long does an installation take?
