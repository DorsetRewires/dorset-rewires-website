Verwood boomed late - what that means for your wiring

Verwood grew faster than almost anywhere in Dorset. A quiet brick-and-pottery village became a town in one long building boom through the 1970s, 80s and 90s. That changes the honest advice, because the town splits three ways:

  • The early boom estates (1960s-70s) are now 50 years old. If a home from then has never been rewired, it is due - old board, no RCD protection, circuits sized for a different life.
  • The 80s and 90s homes - most of Verwood - usually do NOT need a full rewire. The cable is generally sound. What they need is a modern consumer unit and an EICR safety report, at a fraction of the cost.
  • The old village stock - the cottages and bungalows that pre-date the boom - can hide genuinely old wiring behind modern decor. These are the ones worth checking properly.

We will tell you which one you are. A free survey and a quick on-the-spot check, and if the wiring is fine, we say so - selling you a rewire you do not need is exactly what our promise rules out.

Check your board before you check anything else

In a town this new, the board tells you nearly everything. Go and look at yours:

  • No test button anywhere on it - that means no RCD, and on an 80s or early 90s house that is the single thing most worth fixing
  • One test button covering the whole house - common in the 90s. It works, but one fault anywhere puts every light out, including the stairs at night
  • A board crammed full with no spare ways - the reason a charger or a hot tub turns into a bigger job than you expected
  • An RCD that trips now and then for no clear reason - usually a tired circuit rather than a faulty RCD, and it is findable
  • A 1970s home still on its first wiring - the earliest boom estates are the ones genuinely at rewire age now
  • Warm plugs, scorch marks or a buzz from the board - stop using it and call us

Chargers, hot tubs and garden rooms

Verwood homes are newer, with driveways and decent gardens, so this is what people here actually ring about. The pattern is always the same: the thing you want is straightforward, and the board is what says no.

  • An EV charger needs its own protected way at the board. On a full 90s board there is nowhere to put it without changing the board first.
  • A hot tub is a heavy, permanent load on a long outdoor run. It is not an extension lead job, whatever the delivery driver says.
  • A garden room or office needs a proper buried armoured supply with its own isolation, not a spur off the garage.

We quote the board and the new circuit together, as one fixed price, so you are not told halfway through that it needs another job first.

Do you have to move out?

For a full rewire, yes - we rewire empty properties only. It is dusty, noisy work with the power off, and it goes quicker with nobody in. In Verwood that mostly applies to the older village houses rather than the estates.

For smaller jobs you stay put. A new consumer unit, an EICR, an EV charger or a few extra sockets are all done around you, on a day that suits - and in Verwood, the smaller job is very often the right answer.
