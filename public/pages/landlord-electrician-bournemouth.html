The law in plain English

Three rules cover every rented home in England:

  • An EICR at least every 5 years. A qualified electrician inspects and tests the electrical installation and gives you a written report. Tenants get a copy. New tenants get it before they move in.
  • Remedial work within 28 days. If the report says work is needed, it must be done within 28 days - sooner if the report says so.
  • Fines up to £40,000 per breach. The maximum rose from £30,000 with effect from 1 May 2026, and the rules now extend to the social rented sector. Councils can fine per breach, so one neglected property adds up fast.

Selling or re-mortgaging a rental? A satisfactory EICR answers the surveyor's electrical questions before they are asked.

Quick answers

How often does a rental property need an EICR?

At least every 5 years, and you must give the report to tenants. New tenants get it before they move in. If the report requires remedial work, it must be completed within 28 days.

How much can a landlord be fined over an EICR?

Councils can fine landlords up to £40,000 per breach. The maximum rose from £30,000 with effect from 1 May 2026, and the rules now extend to the social rented sector.

How much does a landlord EICR cost in Bournemouth or Poole?

Fixed prices by property size: £160 for a 1-bed flat, £210 for a 2-3 bed, £265 for a 4-5 bed, and £345 for an HMO or large property. No VAT to add. Landlords with 3 or more properties get 10% off.

What happens if the EICR fails?

We price the remedial work on the spot, fix it within the 28-day window, and the retest within 30 days of our remedials is free. You get the paperwork your council or agent needs.

Do you work with letting agents?

What are the smoke alarm rules for landlords in England?

You need a working smoke alarm on every storey with living space, and a carbon monoxide alarm in any room with a fixed fuel-burning appliance. We fit mains-powered alarms at £110 a point, usually in the same visit as your EICR.
