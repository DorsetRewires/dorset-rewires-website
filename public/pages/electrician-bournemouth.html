Bournemouth is a rented town, and that changes the work

More of Bournemouth is let than owned outright compared with the towns around it. Two universities, a town centre full of flats, and street after street of big Victorian houses carved into three or four units. If you are a landlord or you manage property here, most of what you need from an electrician is legal rather than optional.

  • Every rented home needs a valid EICR, renewed at least every five years, and a copy to the tenant. That has been the law for all tenancies in England since April 2021.
  • Anything found at C1 or C2 must be put right within 28 days of the report, or sooner if the report says so, with written confirmation to the tenant and the council if they ask.
  • Licensed HMOs get inspected on it. The certificate is one of the first things asked for, and an expired one holds up a licence.

We work to that timetable rather than against it. Fixed prices by circuit count, the report the same day where we can, and remedial work quoted from the report itself so there are no surprises. See EICR reports, failed EICR remedial work and the landlord page.

What a 1980s conversion actually looks like inside

The single most common Bournemouth job. A large Victorian or Edwardian house near the centre, in Boscombe, Winton or Springbourne, split into flats decades ago and barely touched since.

  • One original supply feeding everything. The split was done cheaply, so flats share circuits they should not, and nobody is quite sure which board owns what.
  • Landlord supply nobody can identify. Hall lights, the stair light, the outside light and sometimes a boiler, all fed from whichever flat had a spare way going.
  • Fire alarm and emergency lighting bolted on later, often onto a circuit that can be switched off from inside a flat.
  • No paperwork at all for the original conversion, which is what makes the first EICR on one of these interesting.

We map what feeds what before quoting. On a converted house that survey is most of the job, and it is the difference between a real price and a guess.

What we find in Bournemouth property

The recurring ones:

  • An old board with no RCD in a flat that has changed hands three times and never been rewired
  • Sockets in the skirting in the terraces of Winton, Charminster and Boscombe still on early wiring
  • Shared circuits between flats, so one tenant can black out another
  • Brittle rubber or fabric-covered cable above the ceilings of the older conversions
  • An expired EICR, or none at all, on a property that is currently let
  • Sockets that scorch, buzz or feel warm - stop using them and call us

Not sure which of these matter? An EICR safety report gives you a written answer, graded by urgency.

Do you have to move out?

For a full rewire, yes - we rewire empty properties only. It is dusty work with the power off. On rented property that means the gap between tenants, and we will work to that date rather than ask you to invent one.

Everything else is done while you live in. A consumer unit upgrade, an EICR, an EV charger or extra sockets are all done around you, on a day that suits. If the wiring is sound and only the fusebox is dated, you may not need a full rewire at all - just a new consumer unit. An EICR tells you which it is.
