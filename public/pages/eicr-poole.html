Poole properties, Poole problems

We test Poole homes every week, and the same patterns come up street by street:

  • Old Town and the Quay. Georgian and Victorian cottages with wiring that has been extended decade after decade. We regularly find old rubber or fabric-covered cable, no earth on the lighting, and boards with no modern shock protection. An EICR tells you exactly where yours stands
  • Parkstone and Branksome semis. Solid 1930s-1960s houses, often on their second or third partial rewire. The usual finds are mixed cable generations, borrowed neutrals and DIY additions from previous owners
  • Sandbanks, Lilliput and Canford Cliffs flats. Apartment living means shared supplies, management companies and access fobs - we handle the freeholder paperwork and can inspect several flats in one visit
  • Hamworthy and Oakdale rentals. A big chunk of Poole's private rental stock. Landlord EICRs, HMO licence renewals and the remedial work, all from one local electrician

Who needs one in Poole

  • Landlords - it is the law. Private landlords in England must have a valid EICR for each rented home, renewed at least every 5 years. The rules have covered all existing tenancies since 1 April 2021
  • HMO licence holders - Poole's HMOs are licensed by BCP Council, and a valid EICR is a standard licence condition. We test, fix and provide the evidence the council wants
  • Holiday-let owners - the harbour, the beaches and the Quay make Poole a holiday-let hotspot. The landlord 5-year law does not cover short lets, but your duty to keep guests safe does, and most insurers want a valid EICR. We recommend one every 5 years
  • Letting agents - portfolio bookings across Poole and the BH postcodes, reports emailed within 24 hours, and one point of contact for the remedial work too
  • Home buyers and sellers - Poole's older stock makes a pre-purchase EICR cheap insurance before money changes hands
  • Homeowners - if your home is 10 years past its last check, or you have just picked up the keys

What we check

An EICR is a full inspection and instrument test against BS7671, the national wiring standard - not a quick look round:

  • The consumer unit (fuse board) - type, condition, and whether it still meets today's safety standards
  • Every circuit - lighting, sockets, cooker, shower and any dedicated circuits, tested from the board
  • Earthing and bonding - the safe path to ground, including the bonding to gas and water pipes
  • RCD protection - the trip devices that cut power in a fraction of a second, tested for real
  • The cabling itself - age, damage, overheating, and the brittle old insulation Poole's older homes love to hide

What happens on the day

  • Time. A one-bed flat is a couple of hours; a typical house about half a day. We give you a realistic slot when you book
  • Power. Some tests need circuits dead, so the power goes off in stretches. We keep it short and warn you first
  • Access. A clear path to the fuse board and reasonable access to sockets. Landlords and agents can just leave a key - flats need the fob sorted, which we arrange with the agent
  • Tenants. We book around them, turn up when we say, and leave the place as we found it

Verdicts and codes, in plain English

Every report ends satisfactory or unsatisfactory. Any C1 (danger now) or C2 (potentially dangerous) makes it unsatisfactory; C3 items are recommendations, not failures, and nobody should pressure you into them to "pass". If your report does fail, we quote every coded item in writing - and our remedial work service can have the faults fixed and certified well inside the landlord 28-day window. If we do the remedial work, the re-test within 30 days is free.

Made safe on the day - our C1 promise

This is where we differ from a test-and-run outfit. If we find a C1 - a danger live in your property right now - we do not photograph it, write it up and drive away.

  • We make it safe before we leave. Isolating the circuit, disconnecting the faulty accessory, whatever removes the risk that day. You are never left living with a known danger while you arrange quotes
  • It is included, not a return visit. Making safe is part of your fixed EICR price. No second call-out, no emergency rate, no invoice you did not expect
  • The report still tells the whole truth. The rules are clear that a C1 must be recorded even once the danger has been removed, so we record it - along with exactly what we did about it on the day. You get an honest document and a safe installation, not one at the expense of the other
  • You get told straight away. A C1 means we tell you on the spot and confirm it in writing immediately, not three days later when the report lands

Any permanent repair after that is quoted in writing and yours to place with whoever you like. Making it safe is not a sales lever - it is just the job.

Quick answers

How much is an EICR in Poole?

It is a fixed price by how many circuits you have: £160 for a 1-bed flat up to 6 circuits, £210 for a 2-3 bed, £265 for a 4-5 bed, and £345 for a large home or HMO of 16 or more circuits. There is no VAT to add.

Do Poole landlords legally need an EICR?

Yes. Private landlords in England must have a valid EICR for each rented property, renewed at least every 5 years, and give the tenant a copy. For a licensed HMO a valid report is also a standard condition of the BCP Council licence.

Do holiday lets in Poole need an EICR?

The landlord 5-year law does not cover short-term lets, but you still have a legal duty to keep the electrics safe and most insurers want a valid EICR. For a holiday let around the harbour we recommend one every 5 years.

How long does the report take?

We send the full PDF report within 24 hours of the inspection.

What if it fails?

We quote any remedial work in writing, itemised against the report's own codes, with no pressure to use us. If we do the work, the re-test within 30 days is free.

Do I need to be there?

No, as long as we can get in and reach the fuse board and the sockets. Many Poole landlords and agents just leave a key or meet us with a fob for apartment blocks. If you want to be there, that is fine too.
